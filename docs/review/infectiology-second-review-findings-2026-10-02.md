# ResusSim — second clinical and antibiotic-stewardship review

**Review date: 2 October 2026.** Reviewed the response log `review-response-2026-10-02.md` and the complete regenerated specification supplied as `review-response-2026-10-02.md2.md`, against the first review and current primary guidance.

**Verdict: substantially improved, but several clinically important rules still need correction before the module is used for assessed physician training.** The principal remaining problems concern treatment eligibility, duration, diagnostic prerequisites and scoring. Correct debrief wording does not establish that the underlying rule has been corrected.

This is a document review, not an execution or source-code audit. Where the response and specification disagree, the finding identifies that disagreement; it does not establish which behavior the running application has. Formula-based findings are deductions from the published model. “High” means a risk of teaching unsafe management or invalidating a central clinical assessment; “medium” means misleading teaching, unfair assessment or an important omission; “low” means wording or presentation. Suggested game deadlines and scoring changes are reviewer judgments, not guideline-prescribed point systems.

Only unresolved or new problems appear below. IDs refer to the regenerated specification; named Part 4 assumptions are used where no item ID exists.

## Missing teaching points and common ward errors

The active safety gaps above should take precedence over adding more organisms or case volume. The following are suitable additions after those rules agree with the intended clinical behavior.

1. **Obstructed infected urinary tract — high.** Add hydronephrosis/obstruction to B1 or B5: urgent urological decompression, effective antibiotics, and the common error of repeatedly broadening treatment while an infected obstruction remains. [IDSA cUTI 2025][CUTI]; [SSC 2026][SSC].
2. **Loading dose, renal recovery and augmented clearance — high.** A patient whose renal function changes should need a new maintenance-dose decision; contrast reduced clearance with augmented clearance. Teach missed loading doses and failure to increase maintenance treatment after renal recovery. [German S3 sepsis 2025][SEPSISDE]; [EUCAST doses][DOSES].
3. **Persistent bacteremia with an initially unrevealing work-up — high.** Add poor-quality TTE, retained intracardiac material or a newly symptomatic joint. The error is declaring the infection uncomplicated from one negative or incomplete investigation. [SAB 2026][SAB26].
4. **True candidemia versus colonization — high.** A3/B3 mostly teach withholding antifungals. Add a contrasting bloodstream-infection case requiring treatment, source assessment, documented clearance and a duration plan linked to clearance and complications. Base the new pathway on the [ECMM/ISHAM/ASM global candidiasis guideline 2025][CANDIDA25].
5. **High-risk prolonged neutropenia and neutropenic enterocolitis — high.** Use a separate case from the existing standard-risk D2. Include persistent fever, new abdominal pain or pulmonary findings, risk-adapted diagnostics and appropriate antifungal escalation; ensure the needed drugs are actually available. [AGIHO FUO update][FN].
6. **Penicillin-allergy assessment — high.** Turn D1’s childhood rash into a teachable history: reaction type, timing, severity and subsequent tolerance. Include a genuine severe-reaction contrast; do not teach either blanket β-lactam avoidance or unqualified re-exposure. [DGAKI and collaborating societies’ position paper, 2025][ALLERGY].
7. **Meningococcal disease and encephalitis uncertainty — high.** Include immediate infection-control/public-health decisions and contact management where indicated; add IV aciclovir when HSV encephalitis remains a relevant differential. [DGN meningitis 2023][MENINGITIS].
8. **Necrotizing soft-tissue infection — high.** A painful rapidly progressive infection should require immediate surgical escalation. Do not let a normal-looking superficial swab, early imaging or antibiotic escalation postpone surgical assessment. [IDSA SSTI guidance][SSTI].
9. **AmpC and mechanism-versus-phenotype interpretation — medium.** A validated E. cloacae isolate should illustrate initially reassuring AST, the risk associated with the selected agent and focus, and why a mechanism comment is different from the laboratory S/I/R category. [IDSA AMR 2026][AMR26]; [EUCAST][BREAKPOINTS].
10. **Drug toxicity, interactions and avoidable combinations — medium.** Examples: duplicate anaerobic coverage; cefepime neurotoxicity during renal impairment; cotrimoxazole-associated hyperkalemia; linezolid monitoring/interactions; rifampicin interactions and monotherapy error. Build specific drug–event pairs rather than a generic random-harm penalty. Use the corresponding product information in each case.
11. **CDI recurrence and diagnostic uncertainty — medium.** Add symptomatic recurrence after apparent recovery, an incidental positive molecular test, and an explicit decision about relapse-prevention strategy and ongoing unnecessary antibiotics/PPI. Avoid turning a positive molecular result into an automatic CDI diagnosis. [DGVS 2023][CDI23]; [IDSA/SHEA diagnostics][CDIDIAG].
12. **Handover and outpatient continuation — medium.** Require the indication, drug/dose/route, effective start/clearance date, next review date, monitoring and a named clinician/team responsible for pending results. Long-course cases need a conditional plan and follow-up, not a stop date alone. [UKHSA stewardship toolkit][SSTF]; assessment-design judgment.

## Clarifications to the first review

I withdraw the earlier implication that old-drain colonizers necessarily enter the polymicrobial cure calculation: the response says causative organisms and colonizers were already separate. I likewise withdraw the concern that the puncture-fluid bottle penalty necessarily applies to CSF; the stated restriction to pleural/ascitic fluid addresses that concern. These were specification ambiguities, not established code defects.

The first review’s TDM recommendation meant **drug- and modality-specific monitoring with early target attainment**, not a universal 24-h laboratory-result deadline. Likewise, unavoidable complications may legitimately affect the patient-outcome axis when clearly labeled; they must not be presented as prescribing errors. A full pharmacokinetic model, universal TEE in every SAB case, and mandatory surgery after every embolus are not prerequisites for this teaching module. The required safeguards are clinically appropriate decisions and consistent assessment.

## Focused acceptance checks for the next revision

These are proposed checks; they have **not** been executed against the application.

- Standard oral azithromycin in an eligible Legionella patient is clinically active; low bioavailability alone does not trigger treatment failure.
- Immediate appropriate antibiotics plus timely catheter removal produce no false “late antibiotic” deduction from the biofilm/source-control modifier.
- A duration accepted by the score does not automatically create a short-course relapse in the same clinical state; include empyema and both meningitis variants.
- E. faecalis endocarditis requires a genuinely adequate regimen and doses; merely adding a ceftriaxone order once does not complete the pathway.
- Persistent SAB triggers additional work-up even outside the named complicated variants; no undocumented-clearance fallback supplies an apparently valid stop date.
- “No LP,” “negative duplex only,” and “fulminant CDI on the standard pathway only” cannot receive complete clinical-management credit.
- A justified empirical/reserve choice is assessed from available information and current indication, including after the AST result.
- The exported MRGN rules, TDM timing and campaign units exactly match the intended runtime rules.
